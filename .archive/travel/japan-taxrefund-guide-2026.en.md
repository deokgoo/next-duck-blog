Online news and social media were flooded with summaries of Japan's tax refund reform ahead of November 1, 2026. However, almost everything was based on official press releases with speculation like "they say it will work like this." Hardly anyone had documented what it was actually like to buy items in stores and scan passports at the new airport kiosks right after the rollout.

Coincidentally, my trip to the Kansai region lined up with the start of November. I experienced the entire process firsthand from day one—shopping at local retailers and completing the refund verification at Kansai International Airport (KIX).

Here is a practical breakdown comparing theory with reality, along with real photos and tips to make your trip seamless.

## 1. End of Point-of-Sale Deduction: Shift to Airport Refund

Starting November 1, 2026, Japan completely overhauled its consumption tax exemption system for foreign tourists.

- **Previous System (until Oct 31, 2026)**: Point-of-sale deduction. Stores directly rang up purchases at the **tax-exclusive price (Tax Free)**, and receipts were reviewed by customs upon departure.
- **New System (from Nov 1, 2026 onwards)**: Pay full price at the register including the **10% consumption tax**, just like local residents. Before flying out, scan your passport at **airport kiosks** to receive a post-departure refund.

The fundamental change is moving from "in-store deduction" to "post-departure airport refund." The policy applies based on your **purchase date**, not your departure date. If your travel dates span late October and early November, purchases made on or after November 1 follow this new protocol.

Arriving at the Kansai Airport departures lobby, large informational displays outlining the new refund procedure were prominently placed throughout the terminal.

![Kansai Airport Tax Refund Information Board](https://storage.googleapis.com/duck-blog.appspot.com/images/taxrefund-2026/taxrefund-notice-board.jpg)

### Abolished Restrictions (A Massive Quality-of-Life Upgrade)

While switching to a refund model adds a step at the airport, several long-standing, frustrating restrictions have been completely scrapped. In daily travel, this was by far the best improvement.

- **No distinction between General Goods and Consumables (消耗品)**: Previously, apparel and packaged snacks had to be rung up separately. Now you can combine everything into a single checkout transaction.
- **Elimination of sealed plastic bags for consumables**: You no longer have to keep snacks, cosmetics, and over-the-counter medicines sealed in tamper-evident plastic bags. You can freely open and use them while traveling in Japan.
- **Removal of the 500,000 JPY daily consumable limit**: The cap has been eliminated, though single purchases exceeding 1,000,000 JPY require individual serial number logging.
- **Departure window extended**: Must depart Japan within **90 days** of purchase (up from 30 days).

## 2. In-Store Shopping and the J-TaxRefund QR Code

I made several purchases after November 1 at major retailers in Osaka's Namba district, including Uniqlo and Bic Camera's specialty liquor store (Bic Shuhan). When you request a tax refund at checkout, a **J-TaxRefund QR code** is printed directly at the bottom of your register receipt.

Scanning the receipt's QR code with your smartphone camera takes you straight to the [J-TaxRefund official mobile web page (https://j-taxrefund.com/)](https://j-taxrefund.com/). Your purchase details and estimated refund amount load automatically. (If the QR code doesn't scan easily, you can open [j-taxrefund.com](https://j-taxrefund.com/) and enter the voucher code manually.)

![J-TaxRefund Mobile Screen - Pending Refund](https://storage.googleapis.com/duck-blog.appspot.com/images/taxrefund-2026/taxrefund-jtaxrefund-waiting.jpg)

Once linked, your transaction appears with an orange badge labeled **[Waiting for Refund / Refund Pending]** (환급 대기). Because you haven't yet passed customs verification at the airport kiosk, this pending status is completely normal.

Most large retailers with updated POS systems print the QR code right on the receipt. Stores still transitioning may hand you a separate paper "J-TaxRefund Voucher."

You only need to complete this registration before your departure flight. You can do it immediately at the store or relax and register your receipts back at your hotel in the evening.

## 3. Registering Your Passport and Refund Card (Pro-Tips)

Opening the J-TaxRefund web page via the QR code guides you through two essential setup steps:

1. **Passport Information**: Using your phone camera to scan your passport's photo page auto-fills your English name, passport number, nationality, and date of birth. Non-Japanese travelers without kanji names are also assigned a system identification number.
2. **Payout Method (Card) Registration**: After confirming your passport details, you are prompted to register the payment card for your refund. The system supports major global and domestic networks including Visa, Mastercard, JCB, Diners Club, Orico, SMBC, and Saison.

> **Crucial Tip**: If you only register your passport and skip registering a payment card, you will have to wait in line at the physical cash refund counter at the airport, where a **10% processing fee is deducted**. Registering both your passport and payout card in advance via the mobile website is the single most important step.

### A Subtle UX Trap: You Cannot Log In with Your Email Address

This was the most confusing friction point I encountered, so take note:

When you first sign up, your session remains logged in on your mobile browser, so everything seems straightforward. But if you close the tab or return a few days later to add receipts from another store, you run into an unexpected hurdle.

Naturally, you would expect to log in with the **email address** you registered with. However, J-TaxRefund does not allow email login.

![J-TaxRefund Login ID Notification Email](https://storage.googleapis.com/duck-blog.appspot.com/images/taxrefund-2026/taxrefund-login-id-email.jpg)

Right after signing up, check your inbox for an email from `no-reply@j-taxrefund.com` titled `[J-TaxRefund] Your Login ID`.

Inside, you will find a **system-generated random alphanumeric ID (e.g., `w8****mz5`)**. **To log back in, you must enter this unique alphanumeric ID, not your email address.**

Because the initial login happens automatically, it is easy to overlook this email, which can lead to unnecessary headache trying to recover your account later.

As soon as you register, **copy your assigned Login ID to your notes app or star/bookmark the confirmation email immediately.**

## 4. Scanning Your Passport at the Airport Kiosk

Once you have completed online registration and linked your card, the airport experience is remarkably fast and frictionless.

Because the system had just launched, I arrived at the airport 3 hours before my flight expecting potential queues. To my surprise, there was **no line at all at the kiosks**, and the entire scan took literally **3 seconds**.

At Kansai International Airport (KIX) Terminal 1, dedicated tax refund kiosk zones are located on the 4th floor International Departures lobby, right between check-in counters E and F.

![Kansai Airport 4th Floor Tax Refund Kiosk Area](https://storage.googleapis.com/duck-blog.appspot.com/images/taxrefund-2026/taxrefund-airport-hall.jpg)

Step up to an available kiosk, follow the on-screen prompts, and **hold your passport's photo page against the scanner for about 3 seconds**.

The screen immediately updates with a confirmation message (`Completed and Approved`), and you are done. There is no need to carry stacks of paper receipts or wait for manual inspection stamps from customs officers.

![Passport Kiosk Verification Approved Screen](https://storage.googleapis.com/duck-blog.appspot.com/images/taxrefund-2026/taxrefund-kiosk-approved.jpg)

### Real-Time Status Change: From [Refund Pending] to [Refund Processing]

Out of curiosity, I refreshed my J-TaxRefund mobile dashboard right after scanning my passport to see if the status updated.

![J-TaxRefund Mobile Screen - Processing Refund](https://storage.googleapis.com/duck-blog.appspot.com/images/taxrefund-2026/taxrefund-jtaxrefund-processing.jpg)

The orange badge previously showing **[Waiting for Refund]** had instantly shifted to a green **[Refund Processing]** status across all transactions (both my purchases from Bic Shuhan and GU updated simultaneously).

Seeing the system update instantly in real time was very reassuring.

I stayed near the kiosk area for about 10 minutes to observe other travelers. Not a single person received a red rejection screen or was flagged for inspection. Everyone placed their passport down and cleared within a couple of seconds. For standard tourists, the chances of random secondary checks appear very low, especially during initial deployment.

Furthermore, approximately 4 dedicated support staff were stationed around the kiosks to assist travelers with any technical hiccups, so you won't be left stranded if you need help.

## 5. Payout Methods: Credit Card vs. Cash Fee Breakdown

Choosing the right payout method makes a substantial difference in the actual amount you receive. Here is how they compare based on actual on-site policies:

- **Credit/Debit Card Refund (Highly Recommended)**: You receive the vast majority of the 10% consumption tax in JPY. It is not 100% fee-free; card networks (such as Visa) charge an international processing fee of **approximately $0.70 USD (around 100–110 JPY)** per batch/transaction, alongside your card issuer's currency conversion rate. (This is noted in the terms when linking your card.)
- **Cash Refund**: **A flat 10% fee is deducted from your refund amount**, and you must physically line up at the **Cash Japan** counter inside the airport terminal.

![Cash Japan Physical Refund Counter](https://storage.googleapis.com/duck-blog.appspot.com/images/taxrefund-2026/taxrefund-cash-japan.jpg)

Opting for cash means losing 10% off the top, plus losing an additional currency exchange margin when converting remaining Yen back to your home currency. Even with the ~$0.70 network fee, credit card payout is vastly more economical. Make sure to register your card before heading to the airport.

## 6. The Most Critical Rule: Complete Before Airline Check-in!

This instruction is highlighted in bold lettering across all airport signage:

> **Complete Tax Refund Before Airline Check-in**

You must complete your kiosk passport scan **before you check your luggage and hand over your suitcase at the airline counter.**

While the vast majority of tourists clear the kiosk with automatic approval, high-value transactions exceeding 1,000,000 JPY or randomly flagged travelers must present their purchased items at a customs inspection counter. If you have already sent your bags down the baggage conveyor belt, customs cannot verify your goods, and your refund will be forfeited. Always visit the kiosks first before heading to your airline check-in line.

## 7. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1. Can I open and consume snacks or cosmetics during my trip in Japan?**  
Yes, absolutely! As of November 1, 2026, the requirement for tamper-evident sealed plastic bags for consumables was abolished. You are free to unwrap, use, and enjoy your purchases while in Japan.

**Q2. Which refund method is better, card or cash?**  
Card is much better. While card refunds carry an international network fee of about $0.70 USD per transaction, cash refunds penalize you with an immediate 10% deduction from your refund total.

**Q3. Are the kiosks located before or after the airport security checkpoint?**  
They are located **before security (landside)**, on the 4th floor International Departures lobby near airline check-in counters. Always visit the kiosk before checking your baggage.
